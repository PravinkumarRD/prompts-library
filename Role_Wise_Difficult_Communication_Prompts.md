# Role-Wise Difficult Communication Prompts

## Short, Action-Oriented and Natural Prompts for Executives

These prompts are designed for executive officers and senior leaders. Each follows a simple, natural structure that can be copied directly into Microsoft Copilot and customised for the situation.

## Simple Prompt Formula

> Act as [Role]. Draft [Communication]. Explain or highlight [Key Requirements]. Use [Tone].

---

# CEO - Difficult Communication

## 1. Business Restructuring

> Act as a CEO Communications Advisor. Draft a message to employees announcing an organisational restructuring, explaining the business rationale, expected benefits, employee support measures, and next steps. Use a transparent and empathetic tone.

## 2. Revenue Miss

> Act as a Strategic Communications Advisor. Draft a communication to shareholders explaining a shortfall in quarterly revenue, highlighting contributing factors, corrective actions, and future growth plans. Use an accountable and confidence-building tone.

## 3. Major Customer Loss

> Act as a CEO Communications Advisor. Draft an email to senior leaders regarding the loss of a key customer, highlighting business impact, lessons learned, recovery plans, and immediate priorities. Use a professional and resilient tone.

## 4. Strategic Initiative Cancellation

> Act as a Strategic Leadership Advisor. Draft a communication informing stakeholders that a major initiative has been discontinued, explaining the reasons, business considerations, and alternative priorities. Use a respectful and transparent tone.

## 5. Crisis Communication

> Act as a Corporate Communications Advisor. Draft an internal communication regarding a business disruption, highlighting known facts, actions underway, employee guidance, and next updates. Use a calm and reassuring tone.

# Universal Difficult Communication Prompt

> Act as an Executive Communications Advisor. Draft a professional communication regarding [issue], acknowledging stakeholder concerns, explaining the business impact, outlining mitigation actions, clarifying next steps, and maintaining confidence in the path forward. Use a transparent and reassuring tone.

## Where to Use This Universal Prompt

Customise it for situations such as:

- ERP or project delays
- Budget reductions
- Customer complaints
- Service disruptions
- Policy changes
- Strategic changes
- Vendor performance issues
- Missed business targets
- Operational incidents
- Organisational restructuring

---

# Difficult Communication Memory Formula

## Acknowledge → Explain → Reassure

A strong difficult communication should:

1. **Acknowledge** the issue and stakeholder concerns.
2. **Explain** the facts, impact, and available context.
3. **Reassure** the audience through clear actions, ownership, and next steps.

## Sample Formula Prompt

> Act as an Executive Communications Advisor. Draft a communication informing [audience] about [difficult situation], acknowledging their concerns, explaining the business impact, outlining mitigation actions, and providing clear next steps. Use a transparent and confidence-building tone.

---

# How to Customise a Prompt

Replace the placeholders or general terms with the specific situation:

- **Audience:** employees, department heads, customers, shareholders, suppliers, managers, or senior leaders
- **Issue:** project delay, budget cut, service outage, missed target, policy change, or quality concern
- **Impact:** operational, financial, customer, workforce, compliance, or reputational impact
- **Actions:** corrective action, mitigation plan, contingency plan, support measure, or escalation
- **Timing:** revised timeline, review date, implementation date, or next update
- **Tone:** empathetic, transparent, firm, diplomatic, accountable, reassuring, or motivating

## Example

### General Version

> Act as an Operations Communications Advisor. Draft a message explaining a significant operational delay, highlighting business impact, root causes, mitigation actions, and revised timelines. Use a professional and solution-oriented tone.

### Customised Version

> Act as an Operations Communications Advisor. Draft a concise email to regional operations heads explaining a four-week warehouse automation delay caused by equipment integration issues, highlighting the impact on fulfilment capacity, key operational risks, temporary workarounds, revised milestones, owners, and next steps. Keep the email under 250 words and use a professional and solution-oriented tone.

# Prompt Quality Check

Before using a difficult communication prompt, confirm that it includes:

1. The appropriate advisory role
2. The communication type and intended audience
3. The difficult issue or decision
4. The business or stakeholder impact
5. Known facts and contributing factors
6. Mitigation or corrective actions
7. Ownership, timing, and next steps
8. An appropriate and respectful tone
9. A requirement not to invent missing facts
10. Human review before the communication is issued

> Keep the prompt short enough to use naturally, but specific enough to produce a responsible, audience-appropriate communication.
