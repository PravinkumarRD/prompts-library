# Executive Short, Action-Oriented and Natural Copilot Prompts

## Purpose

This prompt collection is designed for executive officers and senior leaders. Each prompt uses a short, action-oriented, and natural format that can be copied, customised, and used directly in Microsoft Copilot.

## Simple Prompt Formula

> Act as [Role]. [Task]. Highlight or include [Key Requirements]. Use [Tone].

---

# Email Communication Prompts

## 1. CEO - Strategic Business Update

> Act as a CEO Communications Advisor. Draft a concise email to senior leadership summarising quarterly business performance, highlighting key achievements, major risks, strategic priorities for the next quarter, and leadership actions required. Use a confident and visionary tone.

## 2. CFO - Budget Variance Communication

> Act as a Chief Financial Communications Advisor. Draft a concise email to business leaders explaining significant budget variances for the current quarter, highlighting financial impact, key drivers, corrective actions, and expectations for the next reporting period. Use a professional and data-driven tone.

## 3. COO - Operational Performance Update

> Act as an Operations Leadership Advisor. Draft a concise email to department heads providing an update on operational performance, highlighting achievements, bottlenecks, operational risks, mitigation actions, and immediate next steps. Use a professional and action-oriented tone.

## 4. CHRO - Employee Policy Announcement

> Act as an HR Communications Advisor. Draft a concise email to employees announcing an update to the hybrid work policy, highlighting key changes, business rationale, employee impact, support mechanisms, and implementation timelines. Use an empathetic and informative tone.

## 5. Sales Director - Quarterly Sales Review

> Act as a Sales Communications Advisor. Draft a concise email to regional sales managers summarising quarterly sales performance, highlighting successes, areas requiring improvement, growth opportunities, and priorities for the next quarter. Use a motivating and results-focused tone.

# Microsoft Teams - Calendar Prompts

## 6. Meeting Agenda Creation

> Act as an Executive Chief of Staff. Create a structured agenda for my upcoming leadership meeting, highlighting meeting objectives, discussion topics, decisions required, estimated time allocation, and expected outcomes. Use a professional and focused tone.

## 7. Meeting Preparation

> Act as an Executive Meeting Advisor. Review the meeting details, attendees, previous discussions, and related documents, and prepare a briefing summary highlighting objectives, stakeholder interests, potential risks, discussion points, and recommended questions. Use a strategic and concise tone.

## 8. Post-Meeting Summary

> Act as an Executive Meeting Analyst. Create an executive summary from the meeting notes, highlighting key decisions, action items, owners, deadlines, unresolved issues, and recommended follow-up actions. Use a professional and action-oriented tone.

## 9. Scheduling Conflict Resolution

> Act as an Executive Scheduling Coordinator. Review my calendar and identify scheduling conflicts, competing priorities, and optimisation opportunities. Recommend the best schedule adjustments while minimising business disruption. Use a practical and professional tone.

## 10. Leadership Meeting Follow-Up

> Act as an Executive Communications Advisor. Draft a follow-up communication after a leadership meeting, highlighting key decisions, responsibilities, timelines, dependencies, and immediate next steps. Use a clear and accountable tone.

## 12. Escalation Summary

> Act as an Executive Business Advisor. Summarise the Teams conversation, highlighting key issues, business risks, decisions required, stakeholder concerns, and recommended actions for leadership review. Use a concise and analytical tone.

# How to Customise These Prompts

Replace general terms with details from the actual situation:

- **Audience:** senior leadership, department heads, employees, customers, partners, or project sponsors
- **Topic:** quarterly performance, ERP implementation, policy change, operational issue, project delay, or strategic initiative
- **Time period:** this week, current quarter, next month, or a specific date
- **Required information:** impact, risks, decisions, actions, owners, deadlines, or expected outcomes
- **Tone:** professional, reassuring, confident, empathetic, analytical, motivating, or urgent

## Example Customisation

### General Prompt

> Act as an Executive Communications Advisor. Draft a concise email to department heads explaining a four-week ERP implementation delay, highlighting business impact, risks, mitigation actions, and next steps. Use a professional and confidence-building tone.

### More Specific Version

> Act as an Executive Communications Advisor. Draft a concise email to Finance, Operations, HR, and Sales department heads explaining a four-week ERP implementation delay caused by vendor integration issues, highlighting the impact on user testing and training, key delivery risks, mitigation actions, revised milestones, owners, and next steps. Keep the email under 250 words and use a professional and confidence-building tone.

# Executive Prompt Quality Check

Before using a prompt, confirm that it clearly states:

1. **Role:** Who should Copilot act as?
2. **Task:** What should Copilot create, review, summarise, or recommend?
3. **Context:** What situation, audience, source, or business objective applies?
4. **Output:** What information should be included?
5. **Constraints:** Is there a required length, scope, deadline, or limitation?
6. **Tone:** How should the response sound?

---

## Key Message for Participants

> Keep the prompt short enough to use naturally, but specific enough to guide Copilot towards the required business outcome.
